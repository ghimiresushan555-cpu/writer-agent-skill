# The strategy interview

Ask in this order. Each block is one message, never more than three questions at a time. Push back exactly once per question — twice is an interrogation.

## Block 1 — the reader (ask all three)

1. **Who is the one person you're writing for?** Name the job, the company size, and the thing they're measured on. "Marketers" is not an answer.
2. **What are they reading now instead of you?** Name publications, people, group chats. This tells you the real competition.
3. **Where and when do they read?** Phone on a commute, desktop with docs open, skimming a crowded inbox. It sets the paragraph length before you write one.

## Block 2 — the wedge (ask one at a time)

4. **What do they believe that you think is wrong?** The answer is `positioning.point_of_view`. If they say "nothing, I just want to be useful," ask: *what does everyone in this field get wrong about their own job?*
5. **What do you have that a smart generalist doesn't?** Data, scars, access, a repeatable method, a customer base. Writing quality is not an asset here — everyone has an LLM now.
6. **Who is the closest channel to this one, and what do you do that they don't?** The answer must survive the replaceable test: swap your name for theirs and the sentence should become false.
7. **What will you never cover, even if it gets traffic?** Refusals define a channel faster than ambitions do.

## Block 3 — the promise (ask one at a time)

8. **Finish this: after reading an issue, the reader does ______ differently.** If the answer is "feels informed," push once for a verb.
9. **What's the smallest useful version of an issue?** 600 words? One chart and three paragraphs? This is your floor, not your target.
10. **What would make a reader forward it to a colleague?** Be specific — usually a number they can quote or an argument they can reuse.

## Block 4 — the machine (batch all three)

11. **How often can you ship on your worst week, for three months?** Cadence is a commitment.
12. **How many days before publication do you actually finish?** The honest number, not the aspirational one.
13. **Who else touches a draft — editor, lawyer, co-founder?** Each reviewer adds a day; put it in `lead_time_days`.

## Block 5 — the voice (batch, then sample)

14. **Send me three paragraphs you've written that sound like you.** Best possible input — mine them for tics and rhythm instead of asking abstract questions.
15. **Which five adjectives?** Then convert each into a behaviour: "direct" means "leads with the claim." An adjective that doesn't change a sentence is decoration.
16. **What do you hate in other people's writing?** The banned list, in the user's own words.
17. **Are you the brand or a person?** "We" reads as a company; "I" reads as a practitioner. Pick one; drifting mid-piece is the most common voice failure.

## When the answers are thin

You will sometimes get "I don't know, you decide." That's fine — but do it in the open:

- Make the choice.
- Mark it `# assumed:` in `channel.yaml`.
- Say in the report which two assumptions matter most, and what would change if they're wrong.

Never quietly invent an audience. A wrong audience is worse than an admitted guess, because five downstream skills will faithfully build on it.
