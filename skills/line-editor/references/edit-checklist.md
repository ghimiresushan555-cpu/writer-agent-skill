# Edit checklist

Run in order. Each pass has a different attention span; mixing them is why edits miss things.

## Pass 1 — the contract (structural, whole-piece)

- [ ] Does the piece deliver the spec's **Promise**? Could a reader state it after one read?
- [ ] Is the **Takeaway** in the last third?
- [ ] Is the **Call to action** present and specific? ("Think about your process" is not an action.)
- [ ] Do all **Claims to land** appear?
- [ ] Is any section doing another section's job?
- [ ] Does the introduction earn its length, or is it warm-up?
- [ ] Does the ending summarize instead of landing?
- [ ] Is the shape right for the material (see `draft-writer/references/structures.md`)?

Report structural problems; don't silently restructure.

## Pass 2 — truth (claim by claim)

- [ ] Every number has source + date + unit + baseline
- [ ] Every quote is verbatim, attributed, dated, on the record
- [ ] Zero `needs-check` claims survive
- [ ] Every superlative has a defined universe
- [ ] Every link resolves; no dead or paywalled-without-note links
- [ ] No claim that the brief explicitly lists under `## What we don't know`
- [ ] Causation vs. correlation stated correctly
- [ ] Opinions are framed as opinions
- [ ] Disclosures present (advising relationships, sponsorship, conflicts)

## Pass 3 — voice (against the guide)

- [ ] Person consistent throughout
- [ ] Zero banned words, zero banned openers, zero banned structures
- [ ] Signature moves present (all of them, at least once)
- [ ] Voice adjectives legible — ask: would this adjective's "rules out" column catch anything here?
- [ ] Mean sentence length within 3 words of target
- [ ] Reading level within one grade of target
- [ ] Idiosyncratic, opinionated sentences survived

## Pass 4 — cut (blocks, then sentences, then words)

- [ ] At least 10% cut from the draft; under `max_words`
- [ ] No paragraph restates the previous one
- [ ] No sentence explains what the reader just read
- [ ] No hedge that carries no information
- [ ] No "in order to," "the fact that," "it is important to note"
- [ ] No doubled adjectives ("simple, straightforward")
- [ ] No throat-clearing transitions ("Now, let's look at...")
- [ ] Longest paragraph ≤ 5 sentences

## Pass 5 — rhythm (read aloud, simulated)

- [ ] No three consecutive sentences of similar length and shape
- [ ] No run of five short sentences
- [ ] Every 30+ word sentence has a reason to be that long
- [ ] Subheads say something; skim them alone and the argument survives
- [ ] Anywhere you run out of breath — split
- [ ] The last sentence is the most useful one

## Pass 6 — mechanics

- [ ] Headline and subheads in the channel's capitalisation style
- [ ] Acronyms expanded on first use, then consistent
- [ ] Numbers: numerals 10+, words below 10 (unless data)
- [ ] Ranges use en dashes; em dashes unspaced and sparing
- [ ] Consistent spelling variant (US/UK) throughout
- [ ] Curly quotes and apostrophes if the house style uses them
- [ ] No double spaces, no stray markdown, no broken link syntax
- [ ] Alt text on every image, describing function
- [ ] Byline, date, and disclosure block present

## Pass 7 — the read-through nobody skips

Read the entire piece top to bottom at normal speed, once, without stopping.

Then answer three questions:

1. Where did I get bored? (That's a cut.)
2. Where did I get confused? (That's a missing transition or a definition.)
3. Where did I stop believing? (That's a claim or a strawman.)

Those three answers are worth more than the previous six passes combined. Put them in the change log.
