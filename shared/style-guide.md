# Universal style guide

These rules are channel-independent. They apply to every draft this pack produces, on top of whatever `channel/voice-guide.md` says. When the two disagree, the voice guide wins on *personality*; this guide wins on *craft*.

## 1. The first sentence earns the second

Delete throat-clearing. If the opening can be cut without losing information, it is not an opening — it is a warm-up.

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| "In today's fast-moving world of climate tech, it can be difficult to know where to start." | "Three utilities told me the same thing last quarter: they cannot read our spreadsheet." |
| "We're excited to share some thoughts on pricing." | "We raised prices 30% and lost two customers. Here's what we learned." |

Test: does the first sentence contain a fact, a tension, or a person? If none of the three, rewrite it.

## 2. Concrete nouns, working verbs

- **Abstract → concrete.** "Challenges" → "the invoice that took nine days." "Stakeholders" → "the procurement lead."
- **Nominalizations → verbs.** "We made a decision" → "we decided." "Provision of support" → "we helped."
- **Kill the cushion verbs.** *leverage, utilize, facilitate, enable, empower, unlock, drive, deliver value* — say the thing the verb was hiding.
- **One adverb per paragraph, maximum.** If a verb needs *very*, *really*, or *incredibly*, the verb is wrong.

## 3. Sentence rhythm

Vary length deliberately. A run of long sentences numbs the reader; a short one after them lands.

- Aim for a mean sentence length of 14–20 words for general audiences, 20–24 for technical.
- No more than two sentences in a row of the same length and shape.
- Paragraphs: 1–4 sentences. If a paragraph needs scrolling on a phone, it is two paragraphs.
- Read the piece aloud (or simulate it). Anywhere you run out of breath, cut.

## 4. Structure over decoration

- One idea per section. If you can't name the section in six words, it is two sections.
- Subheads should say something ("Pricing broke the pilot"), not label something ("Pricing").
- Lists: three to five items, parallel grammar, and only when the content is genuinely enumerable. A list of two is a sentence. A list of nine is a document.
- Bold is for the sentence a skimmer should catch, no more than one per section. Never bold a whole paragraph.

## 5. Evidence and hedging

- A number without a source, a date, or a comparison is decoration. Add the unit, the period, and the baseline.
- Prefer "we lost 2 of 11 customers" to "we saw some churn."
- Hedge only when the evidence is genuinely partial, and then name the gap: "based on four interviews, not a survey."
- Never hedge and inflate in the same sentence ("arguably one of the most transformative...").

## 6. Respect the reader's time

- Cut 10% on the first pass, then another 10% if the piece is over 1,500 words. The target is usually *shorter than you think* — a 1,200-word piece that respects attention beats a 2,000-word piece that doesn't.
- Link out instead of summarizing what the reader can read in one click.
- No "as we all know," "it goes without saying," "needless to say," "in conclusion."
- End on the most useful sentence, not the most summarizing one. Skip the recap paragraph if the piece already landed.

## 7. Accessibility and mechanics

- Sentence case for headlines and subheads unless the voice guide says otherwise.
- Expand an acronym on first use; then use it consistently.
- Alt text on every image, describing function, not file name.
- Links carry meaning: "the 2024 grid report," not "click here."
- Em dashes: fine, sparingly, unspaced (—). En dashes for ranges (10–20). No double hyphens.
- Numbers: numerals for 10 and above, words for one through nine — except in data, where numerals always win.

## Quick pre-flight (10 seconds, before any draft leaves)

1. Does sentence one contain a fact, tension, or person?
2. Is there any sentence I'd cut and nobody would notice? Cut it.
3. Does every number have a unit, a period, and a baseline?
4. Can a skimmer get the argument from the subheads alone?
5. Is the last sentence the most useful one?
