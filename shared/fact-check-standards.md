# Fact-check standards

`research-brief` labels claims. `line-editor` refuses to pass a draft with unresolved labels. This file is the rulebook both obey.

## The three labels

| Label | Meaning | May ship? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sourced` | Verified against a named, checkable source, with a date and a link or citation. | Yes |
| `needs-check` | Plausible, load-bearing, but not yet verified. | **No** — blocks publication |
| `opinion` | The author's judgement, analysis, or a clearly-framed prediction. | Yes, if unmistakably framed as such |

Every claim in a brief carries exactly one label. A claim with no label is treated as `needs-check`.

## What counts as a source

Ranked. Prefer higher, permit lower only with a note saying why.

1. **Primary and inspectable** — the filing, dataset, paper, transcript, or documentation itself. Best.
2. **Direct interview or first-hand observation** — a named person who said it to you, on the record.
3. **Reputable secondary reporting** that links to (1) — fine when (1) is behind a paywall; cite both.
4. **Reference works and textbooks** — fine for definitions and settled facts.
5. **Company blogs, press releases, marketing pages** — usable for *what the company claims*, never as neutral fact.
6. **Social posts, forum threads, anonymous sources** — usable only as evidence that a conversation is happening, never as the basis of a factual claim.
7. **Model output or an AI summary** — not a source. Use it to find sources, never to stand in for one.

## Checks every claim gets

- **Date.** When was this true? Numbers older than two years in a fast-moving field need a note.
- **Unit and baseline.** Percent of what? Compared to when? Per what?
- **Direction of causation.** "X correlates with Y" is not "X causes Y." Say which one the evidence supports.
- **Original vs. relayed.** Find the origin. Statistic laundering (a claim cited only by people citing each other) is the most common failure mode.
- **Definition drift.** "Users," "revenue," "active," "success" — do the source and the draft mean the same thing?
- **Quote accuracy.** Verbatim, attributable, in context, and with the speaker's role and date. If you cannot verify the exact wording, paraphrase and say so.

## Specific failure modes to hunt

- **The confident number.** A precise figure with no methodology ("87% of teams report..."). Trace it or cut it.
- **The dead link.** Verify every URL resolves at draft time, not at research time.
- **The unearned superlative.** "First," "fastest," "only," "largest" — requires a defined universe and a date.
- **The silent range collapse.** "Studies show" covering three contradicting studies.
- **The quote without a speaker.** Attribute every quote to a named person and a role.
- **The borrowed authority.** "Experts agree" with no expert named.

## Corrections

When an error ships, the correction is a first-class piece of writing: what was wrong, what's right, what changed, when. No silent edits to published pieces. The correction goes at the top, in plain language, with the date.
